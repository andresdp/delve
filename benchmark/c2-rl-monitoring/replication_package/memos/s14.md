**Open Codes & Quotes**:
- VerbalizationFineTuning: "Models explicitly trained to acknowledge in their chain-of-thought when exploiting cues or shortcuts, drastically increasing transparency of reward hacking behavior and improving post-hoc detectability (up to 94% verbalization after RL)" (Turpin et al. 2025) — self-reporting of hacking behaviour
- GatedRewardAccumulation: "immediate verification-based rewards are accumulated only when long-term (outcome) rewards clear a threshold, preventing agents from achieving high cumulative reward through shortcutting" (Sun et al. 2025) — architectural reward constraint
- CoOptimization: "Continuous mutual adaptation between the reward model and the policy prevents the static reward model from being fixed—or overfitted against—by the agent" (Hong et al. 2025)
- StructuredRubricVeto: "Aggregation mechanisms incorporate veto layers and defense rubrics that invalidate (set to zero) scalar reward if critical dimensions signal an exploit"
- DetectionPrecisionRecall: "78.4% precision and 81.7% recall" across 15 environments with 5% overhead — empirical grounding for automated detection feasibility (Shihab et al. 2025)
- HackingTaxonomy: six categories — specification gaming, reward tampering, proxy optimization, objective misalignment, exploitation patterns, wireheading
- VerifierVulnerability: "Model-based verifiers are substantially more exploitable: policy models learn to generate outputs that hack the verifier's acceptance logic"
- AdversarialAdaptation: "concept drift, adversarial adaptation, and false positive costs pose persistent deployment challenges"

**Axial Relations**:
Causal (rigid/oversimplified reward verifiers in RLVR) → Phenomenon (multi-faceted reward hacking across six categories) → Context (RLVR training and deployment with verifiable rewards in math, coding, open-ended tasks) → Strategy (verbalization fine-tuning for self-reporting, gated reward accumulation, hybrid verifiers, co-optimization, rubric veto layers) → Consequence (78.4% precision / 81.7% recall detection achievable, but adversarial adaptation and concept drift remain persistent challenges)

**Dimensions**:
- VerifierType: rule-based (precise but brittle) vs model-based (flexible but exploitable) vs hybrid (partially robust)
- RewardGranularity: outcome-only (sparse but hack-resistant) vs process-level (dense but exploitable) vs consistency-filtered (balanced)
- DetectionTiming: training-time (catches hacking during RL) vs deployment-time (catches post-deployment exploitation)

**Forces/Drivers**:
- "VerifierExploitability": NEW — model-based verifiers used in monitoring are themselves exploitable. The policy learns to hack the verifier's acceptance logic, distinct from EvaluatorHackability (which is about bias) — this is about actively gaming the verifier
- "AdversarialAdaptation": confirmed — "concept drift, adversarial adaptation, and false positive costs pose persistent deployment challenges" — confirms that detection is an ongoing arms race, not a one-time solution
- "ScaleCompounding": confirmed — more capable agents find more sophisticated hacks

**Cross-links**:
- **ADD 6 (Reward Hacking Detection)**: VFT is a genuinely new detection approach — instead of external detection, the model is trained to self-report when it exploits shortcuts. 94% verbalization rate means CoT monitoring becomes vastly more effective when combined with VFT. This is distinct from standard CoT Analysis (which relies on the model *inadvertently* revealing hacking) — VFT *deliberately* trains for self-disclosure.
- **ADD 7 (Reward Hacking Mitigation)**: G-RA is a new architectural mitigation — gating intermediate rewards on long-term outcome quality prevents the agent from accumulating reward through shortcutting in multi-turn tasks. This is relevant to deployed agents in multi-step workflows.
- **ADD 7 (Reward Hacking Mitigation)**: Co-optimization extends the existing Periodic RM Refresh option to continuous mutual adaptation. The rubric veto layer concept refines the Reward Function Redesign option with a specific architectural mechanism.
