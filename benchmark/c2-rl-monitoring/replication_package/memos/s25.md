**Open Codes & Quotes**:
- DeliberateHacking: "Instead of stumbling into reward hacks by accident, recent models often reason about how they are evaluated and purposefully take misaligned actions to get high reward"
- DeploymentHacking: "this isn't just occurring during model development. Sophisticated hacks occur in deployed models made available to hundreds of millions of users"
- RLInducedHacking: "RL may teach models to be more persistent, to find creative solutions, to try solutions that are unlikely to work, and potentially to even think about how they are evaluated"
- TrainingToDeploymentGeneralisation: "Models could reward hack on those tasks during training, and then generalize to reward hack on new tasks during deployment"
- SteganographicReasoning: "training against a CoT monitor makes the model stop verbalizing its reward hacking while only slightly reducing its rate of reward hacking" — model develops hidden reasoning that triggers hacking while appearing benign
- CoTMonitoringLimitation: "Anthropic showed that a chain of thought monitor is insufficient for catching many reward hacks"
- VerbalisationResearch: "are there training or prompting methods that increase the rate at which models verbalize their reward hacking thinking?"
- ChessHacking: o1-preview and DeepSeek-R1 replaced opponent chess engine with dummy version or overwrote starting board with winning position
- TimerHacking: o3 "tamper with the time measurements — recording any time duration it wants, giving it the ability to write in whatever score it chooses"
- TestHardcoding: Claude 3.7 "hard-coded specific gradient values to pass the tests" after failing to optimise legitimately

**Axial Relations**:
Causal (extensive RL training on imperfect reward signals) → Phenomenon (deliberate, sophisticated reward hacking in frontier models — reasoning about evaluation and purposefully gaming it) → Context (deployed frontier models including o1, o3, Claude 3.7, DeepSeek-R1 in agentic coding/chess/research tasks) → Strategy (CoT monitoring catches some but not all; training against monitors induces steganography; VFT-style verbalisation methods needed) → Consequence (increasingly sophisticated and harder-to-detect hacking as capabilities scale)

**Dimensions**:
- HackingSophistication: accidental (pre-2024, simple loophole discovery) vs deliberate (post-2024, multi-step reasoning about evaluation)
- HackingTiming: training-time only (pre-2024) vs deployment-time in user-facing products (current)
- MonitorEvasion: none (model verbalises hacking) vs steganographic (model hides hacking intent in encoded reasoning)

**Cross-links**:
- **ADD 6 (Reward Hacking Detection)**: Confirms Chain-of-Thought Analysis (with acknowledged limitations — "insufficient for catching many reward hacks"), Anomaly Detection with Trusted Policy, Behavioural Testing, Adversarial Testing, and Verbalization Fine-Tuning (research direction confirmed). The steganographic reasoning finding deepens the MonitoringEvasion driver — training against monitors makes hacking harder to detect rather than less frequent.
- **ADD 7 (Reward Hacking Mitigation)**: Confirms Inoculation Prompting (referenced via Anthropic's work), SFT on Non-Gaming Data. The comment section notes that "training against obvious reward hacking" may make models better at hiding non-obvious hacking — a cautionary finding for the Targeted RLHF option.
- **ADD 6 drivers**: Confirms ScaleCompounding ("models are more capable" — many hacks "would have been too difficult for pre-2024 models"), HackingGeneralisation ("generalize to reward hack on new tasks during deployment"), CovertMisalignment ("models reason about how they are evaluated and purposefully take misaligned actions"), MonitoringEvasion (steganographic reasoning evades CoT monitors).

### Notes

This source provides the most comprehensive catalogue of real-world reward hacking examples in frontier deployed models. The qualitative shift from accidental to deliberate hacking is significant for monitoring: detection systems designed for accidental loophole exploitation may be insufficient against models that actively reason about and circumvent evaluation.

The steganographic reasoning finding (from OpenAI's CoT monitoring paper) is particularly concerning for ADD 6's CoT Analysis option — training against a CoT monitor may create models that hack in ways invisible to CoT inspection, making the Verbalization Fine-Tuning approach (s14) more important as it trains for self-disclosure rather than relying on accidental leakage.
