**Open Codes & Quotes**:
- CoTMonitoringEffectiveness: "We can monitor their thinking with another LLM and effectively flag misbehavior"
- WeakerMonitorsStronger: GPT-4o (weaker model) effectively monitors a frontier reasoning model stronger than o3-mini — "promising evidence that CoT monitoring may provide a new avenue for scalable oversight, where trusted weaker models help to oversee superior, even superhuman, models"
- CoTSuperiorToAction: "Our monitor is far more effective if it has access to the agent's chain-of-thought in addition to the agent's actions, indicating that the intent to reward hack can be easier to detect in the CoT than in the agent's actions alone"
- NaturalForthriteness: "It's common for frontier reasoning models to very clearly state their intent within their chain-of-thought. For example, they are often so forthright about their plan to subvert a task they think 'Let's hack'"
- ObfuscationFromPressure: "directly optimizing the CoT to adhere to specific criteria may boost performance in the short run; however, it does not eliminate all misbehavior and can cause a model to hide its intent"
- FragileMonitorability: "Their natural monitorability is very fragile. If strong supervision is directly applied to the chain-of-thought, models can learn to hide their intent while continuing to misbehave"
- RecommendUnrestricted: "We recommend against applying strong optimization pressure directly to the CoTs of frontier reasoning models, leaving CoTs unrestricted for monitoring"
- ScalableOversight: "CoT monitoring may be one of the few effective methods we have for supervising superhuman models"

**Axial Relations**:
Causal (frontier reasoning models trained with RL exploit loopholes in coding tasks) → Phenomenon (reward hacking detectable via natural-language CoT — models explicitly state intent to hack) → Context (OpenAI frontier RL training run, model stronger than o3-mini) → Strategy (GPT-4o as prompted CoT monitor, detecting hacking intent in reasoning traces) → Consequence (effective real-time detection, but fragile — CoT pressure causes obfuscation, trading detection capability for marginal capability gain)

**Dimensions**:
- MonitorAccess: actions only (Action Monitor — less effective) vs CoT + actions (CoT Monitor — far more effective)
- CoTOptimisation: unrestricted (monitorable, recommended) vs pressured (obfuscated, dangerous)
- MonitorCapability: weaker than agent (GPT-4o monitoring frontier model — still effective) vs equal/stronger

**Cross-links**:
- **ADD 6 (Reward Hacking Detection)**: Definitive primary source for Chain-of-Thought Analysis option. Key empirical findings: (1) CoT monitoring catches hacks that action-only monitoring misses; (2) GPT-4o can monitor a stronger model; (3) all shown examples were successfully flagged. Also confirms the critical limitation: CoT pressure causes obfuscation, making MonitoringEvasion driver concrete.
- **ADD 6 → ADD 7 interaction**: Critical architectural recommendation — do NOT use CoT monitoring as a training reward signal (ADD 7 mitigation) because this destroys its effectiveness as a detection tool (ADD 6). This creates a fundamental tension: the most effective detection method is degraded if used for mitigation. The recommendation is to keep detection and mitigation architecturally separate.
- **ADD 7 (Reward Hacking Mitigation)**: Confirms that penalising "bad thoughts" is counterproductive — "the agent with CoT pressure still cheats, albeit at a lower rate, and almost all of its cheating is undetectable by the monitor." Supports Inoculation Prompting (which doesn't suppress CoT) and MONA (which limits horizon rather than monitoring CoT) over direct CoT optimisation.
