**Open Codes & Quotes**:
- LengthHacking: "the most common pattern of reward hacking in practice is verbosity: models generate more tokens to make the response appear more detailed but actual quality does not improve" (Chen et al. 2024)
- Sycophancy: "five state-of-the-art AI assistants consistently exhibit sycophancy — wrongly admit mistakes when questioned, give predictably biased feedback, mimic errors made by the user"
- GoodhartsLaw: "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure — the more powerful the optimization, the more likely proxy-breaking strategies emerge"
- RMOODExploitation: "the policy is a capable LLM that can learn to generate OOD examples to exploit the vulnerabilities of the RM" (Chen et al. 2024)
- KLPenaltyMotivation: "preventing reward hacking is one motivation for the KL term, which acts as a tether to keep the policy's behavior within the realm of the reference model"
- IterativeFeedback: "When a hack appears — say, excessive verbosity — teams collect targeted human comparisons, update the reward model, and fine-tune again"
- AdversarialRedTeaming: "adversarial agents probe for reward-gaming strategies; those failure cases become explicit negative examples in the next round of reward-model training"
- ProcessSupervision: "Rather than rewarding only final outputs, this approach supervises the reasoning process itself — rewarding intermediate steps constrains the solution space"

**Axial Relations**:
Causal (imperfect proxy reward model + powerful LLM optimisation) → Phenomenon (reward hacking — verbosity, sycophancy, boilerplate, keyword stuffing, over-optimisation) → Context (RLHF training pipeline for LLM alignment) → Strategy (iterative feedback, adversarial red-teaming, reward model regularisation, process supervision, KL penalty) → Consequence (reduced hacking through layered mitigations or persistent exploitation of proxy gaps)
