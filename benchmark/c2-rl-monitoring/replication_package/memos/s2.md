## Memo 2: Reward Deterioration Detection via Statistical Testing (Source 2)

**URL**: https://towardsdatascience.com/detecting-rewards-deterioration-in-reinforcement-learning-92338e8740d1/  
**Paper**: Greenberg & Mannor, "Detecting Rewards Deterioration in Episodic Reinforcement Learning", ICML 2021

**Open Codes & Quotes**:
- PostDeploymentFocus: "in many applications, the training has to stop and the agent has to be fixed before deploying it to run on production. In such frameworks, it is essential to be aware of the performance of the agent at any point of time"
- SafetyCriticality: "it is critical to know ASAP if the performance begins to deteriorate (e.g. if the car controller starts faltering, we'd like to notice before it actually crushes into anything)"
- ExternalMonitoring: "our monitoring system is entirely external and independent of the agent — does not require model-based learning"
- ReferenceDataset: "we assume that a reference dataset of 'valid' rewards is available (in real applications this could be the recording of the tests of the system)"
- NaiveSuboptimality: "just take the mean reward over the recent episodes, and compare it to the reference. However, it turns out that this is highly sub-optimal"
- CovarianceWeighting: "an optimal test would consider a weighted mean of the rewards rather than a simple mean, with weights corresponding to the row-sums of the inverse covariance matrix"
- NonIIDEpisodic: "rewards within each episode are not independent, nor identically-distributed, nor Markov"
- BFAR: "Bootstrap for False Alarm Rate control in non-i.i.d signals — during h episodes of sequential tests, the probability of a false-alarm must not exceed alpha"
- OrdersOfMagnitude: "our suggested test detected degradation faster and more often than the alternative tests — often by orders of magnitude"
- CompetitorTests: "competitors: the naive simple mean (usually used in RL in practice), CUSUM (from sequential testing), and Hotelling (from multivariate mean-shift testing)"
- EnvironmentModification: "we trained an agent on several environments, then made changes to the environments and let the rewards deteriorate (with no further training)"
- MultipleInfoSources: "in RL we have several characteristic sources of information: the rewards, the observations, and the understanding of the agent (e.g. value-function)"
- SafetyActivation: "Once degradation is detected, corresponding safety mechanisms can be activated — control may pass to a human driver; the patient may be referred to a doctor; a dump may be sent to the developers"

**Axial Relations**:
Causal (environment modification / non-stationarity after deployment) → Phenomenon (reward signal deterioration in deployed RL agent with fixed policy) → Context (safety-critical episodic production settings where the agent must not be modified while running) → Strategy (covariance-weighted mean-shift test on reward signal compared to reference dataset, with BFAR for false alarm control) → Consequence (early degradation detection enabling safety activation, or late/missed detection with potentially catastrophic outcomes)

**Dimensions**:
- TestStatistic: naive mean (common practice, sub-optimal) vs covariance-weighted mean (provably optimal under normality) vs CUSUM (sequential) vs Hotelling (multivariate)
- FalseAlarmControl: fixed threshold (assumes stationarity) vs BFAR bootstrap (handles non-i.i.d. episodic data)
- MonitoringCoupling: external/model-free (this paper) vs internal/model-dependent (requires access to agent internals)
- MonitoringSignal: rewards (this paper) vs observations vs agent value-function estimates
- DetectionSpeed: orders of magnitude faster (weighted) vs standard speed (naive)
- EpisodicAssumption: episodic (required by this method) vs continuous (not supported)

**Forces/Drivers**:
- "DetectionLatency": in safety-critical systems (autonomous vehicles, medical devices), the time between degradation onset and detection is the critical metric — faster detection prevents harm
- "FalseAlarmCost": false alarms erode operator trust and trigger unnecessary interventions; BFAR provides formal control over false alarm probability
- "SignalNonStationarity": rewards within episodes are correlated and non-identically-distributed, so standard i.i.d. statistical tests are invalid — specialized episodic methods are required
- "EpisodicStructure": the method exploits episode boundaries for bootstrapping, but this limits applicability to non-episodic continuous production settings
- "ReferenceDatasetAvailability": the method requires a pre-collected dataset of "valid" rewards as baseline — this must be representative of normal operation
- "MonitoringIndependence": external monitoring that does not require modifying the agent or knowing its internals is valuable for black-box deployed policies

**Cross-links**:
- Extends ADD 2 (Production Monitoring Signal Selection) from S1: this source provides a specific, formally grounded method for the "RL-specific metric tracking" option, specialising it to reward signals with covariance weighting.
- The "safety activation" consequence connects to ADD 3 (Automated Response Mechanism) from S1: once degradation is detected, the system must decide how to respond (circuit breaker, rollback, HITL).
- The "environment modification" cause aligns with S1's "EnvironmentNonStationarity" driver, but this source provides the detection mechanism rather than the response mechanism.
- Multiple monitoring signals (rewards, observations, value-function) noted but only rewards addressed — later sources may address observation-level or transition-level drift detection.

## ADD 4: Reward Degradation Test Statistic Selection (Source 2 Primary)

**Description**: Choose the statistical test for detecting reward signal deterioration in a deployed RL agent with a fixed policy. The decision trades off detection power (how quickly and reliably degradation is detected) against assumptions required (normality, episodic structure) and implementation complexity. The naive mean comparison is standard practice but provably sub-optimal; the covariance-weighted test is optimal under normality and outperforms alternatives by orders of magnitude empirically.

| Decision Options | Source Quote | Memo Link |
|---|---|---|
| Covariance-Weighted Mean Test | "an optimal test would consider a weighted mean of the rewards, with weights corresponding to the row-sums of the inverse covariance matrix" | Memo 2 |
| Naive Simple Mean | "just take the mean reward over the recent episodes — this is highly sub-optimal" | Memo 2 |
| CUSUM Sequential Test | "CUSUM from sequential testing" — a standard sequential change-detection method | Memo 2 |
| Hotelling Multivariate Test | "Hotelling from multivariate mean-shift testing" — standard multivariate test | Memo 2 |
| Embedding-Based Drift Detection | "embedding-based drift detection — analyzing embedding clusters over time" — detects environment change before reward manifests | Memo 8 (S8) |
| PSI / KL Input Distribution Test | "Population Stability Index and KL Divergence quantify how much new inputs deviate" — monitors state/observation distributions | Memo 8 (S8) |

| Decision Drivers (Forces) | Impact | Source Quote |
|---|---|---|
| DetectionLatency | +CovarianceWeighted (orders of magnitude faster) | "detected degradation faster — often by orders of magnitude" |
| SignalNonStationarity | +CovarianceWeighted (accounts for correlated, non-i.i.d. rewards) | "rewards are not independent, nor identically-distributed, nor Markov" |
| EpisodicStructure | +CovarianceWeighted (exploits episode structure), -CUSUM (designed for i.i.d.) | "episodic framework" |
| ReferenceDatasetAvailability | Required for all options | "reference dataset of 'valid' rewards is available" |
| MonitoringIndependence | Positive for all (external test) | "entirely external and independent of the agent" |
| GoodhartsLaw | Negative for reward-only tests (reward can be gamed upward) | "any metric used as target will be gamed" — Memo 5 (S5). Implies ADD 4 is necessary but not sufficient; must combine with ADD 6 |
| DriftInevitability | +EmbeddingDrift, +PSI (proactive detection before reward impact) | "models left unchanged for 6+ months saw error rates jump 35%" — Memo 8 (S8) |
| RewardInflationRisk | Fundamental limitation of all reward-only tests | "reward hacking causes reward to increase/remain stable while true performance degrades" — ADD 4 detects downward only; upward gaming requires ADD 6 — Memo 6 (S6) |

## ADD 5: False Alarm Rate Control Method (Source 2 Primary)

**Description**: Choose how to set detection thresholds to control the rate of false alarms in sequential online reward monitoring. The decision trades off false alarm control rigour against assumptions about the data. BFAR provides bootstrap-based control that is valid for non-i.i.d. episodic data; fixed thresholds assume stationarity and fail for correlated signals; adaptive thresholds provide online adjustment but lack formal guarantees.

| Decision Options | Source Quote | Memo Link |
|---|---|---|
| Bootstrap FAR (BFAR) | "Bootstrap for False Alarm Rate control in non-i.i.d signals — during h episodes, probability of false-alarm must not exceed alpha" | Memo 2 |
| Fixed Statistical Threshold | Standard parametric threshold assuming i.i.d. data | Memo 2 |
| Adaptive Threshold | Online threshold adjustment based on observed statistics | Memo 2 |
| Variance-Tuned Threshold | "Use thresholds tuned to historical variance to avoid false positives" — calibrate to observed noise level | Memo 4 (S4) |

| Decision Drivers (Forces) | Impact | Source Quote |
|---|---|---|
| FalseAlarmCost | +BFAR (formal control) | "false alarms were successfully controlled — indicates the success of BFAR" |
| SignalNonStationarity | +BFAR, -Fixed (i.i.d. assumption violated) | "sampling in the bootstrap may ruin the distribution" of non-i.i.d. signals |
| EpisodicStructure | +BFAR (exploits episode i.i.d. assumption) | "relies on the assumption that even though the time-steps are not i.i.d, the episodes are" |
| FlappingRisk | +BFAR, +VarianceTuned, -Fixed (aggressive thresholds cause rollback flapping) | "setting thresholds too aggressively leads to flapping — constant rollback/redeploy" — Memo 7 (S7) |
| GovernanceTraceability | +BFAR, +VarianceTuned (formal guarantees for audit) | BFAR and similar methods with documented FAR control support governance and compliance requirements |
