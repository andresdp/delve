**Open Codes & Quotes**:
- TrainingComplianceVsDeploymentResilience: "RL safety does not usually fail because engineers forgot to care about safety. It fails because they confuse training compliance with deployment resilience"
- ConditionalSafety: "a constraint that works on the training distribution is not automatically a constraint that survives distribution shift"
- ShieldInterventionRate: "Intervention rate under shift is one of the first signals that your safety envelope has stopped matching reality" — monitoring how often the safety shield blocks actions as a drift proxy
- HiddenVariableBlindness: "Hidden parameters such as friction, latency, actuator wear, patient sensitivity, or sensor calibration can change the true risk of the same visible state-action pair"
- LaggingMultiplier: "The multiplier reacts to observed violations. Under fast distribution shift, that is a lagging signal. By the time costs spike, the policy may already be operating in unfamiliar territory"
- AdaptiveShielding: "static shields do not transfer cleanly across changed dynamics" — shields must adapt online to new dynamics, not assume fixed models
- OfflineCoverageGap: "if your logs barely contain near-boundary failures, your agent may never learn where the cliff actually is"
- RobustnessIsNotMagic: "If the real shift lands outside the modeled family, robust optimization gives a false sense of completeness. Robustness is not magic. It is coverage with a mathematical accent"
- SaferStack: "conservative policy learning, explicit uncertainty estimation, support-aware offline evaluation, adaptive shielding, out-of-distribution detection, stress testing on shifted environments, and deployment metrics focused on safety generalization"

**Axial Relations**:
Causal (distribution shift in deployment — hidden variables change, dynamics drift, cost models become inaccurate) → Phenomenon (seven categories of safety constraint failure in deployed RL agents) → Context (safety-critical production environments where environment refuses to sit still) → Strategy (layered monitoring: OOD detection + adaptive shielding + shield intervention rate tracking + stress testing) → Consequence (deployment resilience or false sense of safety from training-time constraint satisfaction)

**Dimensions**:
- SafetyType: hard boundary (shield/constraint) vs soft trade-off (reward penalty)
- ShiftSpeed: slow (Lagrange can adapt) vs fast (lagging signal, constraint fails before adjustment)
- VariableVisibility: observed state (constraint seems met) vs hidden variable (true risk changed undetected)
- ShieldAdaptivity: static (built for training distribution) vs adaptive (infers new parameters online)

**Forces/Drivers**:
- "EnvironmentNonStationarity": confirmed and deepened across all seven failure modes
- "HiddenVariableShift": NEW — hidden parameters (friction, latency, actuator wear, patient sensitivity, sensor calibration) change the true risk of the same visible state-action pair. The observed state looks safe but the hidden context makes it dangerous. Distinct from general EnvironmentNonStationarity because the observable signals show no drift — the shift is invisible to standard monitoring
- "FalseAlarmCost": confirmed — "robust constraints defend the wrong shift" and shields "over-block safe" actions under shift
- "DetectionLatency": confirmed — Lagrange multiplier is a lagging signal, too slow for fast shift

**Cross-links**:
- **ADD 2 (Production Monitoring Signal Selection)**: Shield intervention rate is a new monitoring signal. If the safety shield starts blocking more actions than baseline, it indicates the learned policy is proposing actions that are unsafe under current conditions — a proxy for distribution shift that doesn't require direct environment modelling.
- **ADD 3 (Automated Response Mechanism)**: Confirms Circuit Breakers and distinguishes static vs adaptive shielding. The source argues that static shields (our current Circuit Breakers option) fail under shift and must be made adaptive. This is an important refinement — the circuit breaker must be updated as the environment changes, not a fixed threshold.
- **ADD 4 (Reward Degradation Test Statistic)**: The hidden-variable blindness concept reveals a limitation — reward-based monitoring (ADD 4) may show no degradation even though true risk has increased, because the agent achieves the same reward in a now-dangerous way. This is analogous to the RewardInflationRisk driver.
- **ADD 5 (False Alarm Rate Control)**: The Lagrange multiplier failure confirms that reactive threshold adjustment is too slow — proactive methods (BFAR, variance-tuned) are needed. The "over-blocking" failure of robust constraints connects to false alarm cost.
- **The layered architecture** (OOD monitor → adaptive shield → safe action gate) maps directly onto our ADD pipeline: ADD 2 (monitoring signals) → ADD 4/6 (detection) → ADD 3 (response) → ADD 5 (threshold calibration).
