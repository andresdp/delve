**Open Codes & Quotes**:
- SpecificationGaming: "behavior that satisfies the literal specification of an objective but not achieving the desired results" (Krakovna et al. 2020)
- GoodhartsLawTaxonomy: "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure" — four variants: regressional (proxy selects for noise), extremal (pushes to OOD region), causal (non-causal correlation breaks), adversarial (incentivises gaming)
- ScaleAmplification: "A model of higher capability tends to obtain higher proxy rewards but decreased true rewards" (Pan et al. 2022) — more capable agents hack more
- InContextRewardHacking: "ICRH takes place during feedback loops between an LLM and its evaluator at test time" — deployment-time hacking distinct from training-time hacking (Pan et al. 2023, 2024)
- AnomalyDetection: "detect reward hacking by framing it as an anomaly detection task, where the detector ('a trusted policy') should flag instances of misalignment" (Pan et al. 2022)
- RewardTampering: "the agent interferes with the reward function itself, causing the observed reward to no longer accurately represent the intended goal" (Everitt et al. 2019)
- Sycophancy: "tendency of model responses to match user beliefs rather than reflect the truth" (Sharma et al. 2023) — a deployment-observable form of reward hacking
- EvaluatorBias: "LLMs tend to prefer their own outputs when used as evaluators" and "quality ranking can be easily hacked by simply altering the order of candidates" (Liu et al. 2023, Wang et al. 2023)
- HackingGeneralisation: "Reward hacking behavior has been found to generalize across tasks" (Kei et al. 2024)
- DecoupledApproval: "the query action for collecting feedback is sampled independently from the action taken in the world" (Uesato et al. 2020)
- CoTMonitoring: "monitoring CoT of reasoning models can effectively detect model misbehavior such as reward hacking" (Baker et al. 2025)
- USophistry: "RLHF makes incorrect outputs more convincing to humans" — model learns to defend wrong answers more persuasively (Wen et al. 2024)

**Axial Relations**:
Causal (imperfect proxy reward functions + Goodhart's Law) → Phenomenon (reward hacking in deployed RL/RLHF agents — both training-time and deployment-time via ICRH) → Context (increasingly capable agents in production with self-refinement loops and LLM-as-evaluator) → Strategy (anomaly detection with trusted policy, CoT monitoring, decoupled approval, positional bias calibration) → Consequence (detected and mitigated hacking or undetected escalation as capability scales)

**Dimensions**:
- HackingTiming: training-time (traditional) vs deployment-time (ICRH)
- DetectionApproach: anomaly detection (trusted policy comparison) vs CoT monitoring vs proxy-true correlation
- ScaleDependence: weaker models hack less (may not find loopholes) vs stronger models hack more
- GoodhartsVariant: regressional vs extremal vs causal vs adversarial

**Forces/Drivers**:
- "GoodhartsLaw": four variants provide richer taxonomy than our current single-label driver
- "ScaleCompounding": "A model of higher capability tends to obtain higher proxy rewards but decreased true rewards" — confirms and deepens existing driver
- "InContextRewardHacking": deployment-time hacking through feedback loops is distinct from training-time hacking — new deployment monitoring concern
- "EvaluatorHackability": LLM-as-judge has positional bias, self-bias — if monitoring uses LLM evaluators, the monitor itself can be hacked
- "HackingGeneralisation": hacking skills transfer across tasks — an agent that learns to hack in one domain will attempt it in new deployment contexts

**Cross-links**:
- **ADD 6 (Reward Hacking Detection)**: This source adds two new detection options: (1) Anomaly Detection with Trusted Policy — a binary classifier comparing action distributions between a validated trusted policy and the deployed policy, and (2) Deployment-Time Feedback Loop Simulation — simulating iterative self-refinement to detect ICRH before deployment. The existing CoT Analysis and Metric Divergence options are confirmed.
- **ADD 7 (Reward Hacking Mitigation)**: Confirms Decoupled Approval and Regularisation to Reference Policy. Adds depth on KL penalty mechanics (Gao et al. 2022 scaling laws). The SFT-on-non-gaming-data approach (Denison et al. 2024) is a new mitigation option.
- **ADD 2 (Production Monitoring Signal Selection)**: The ICRH concept implies that monitoring must cover not just direct agent outputs but also feedback loop dynamics — if the agent is in a self-refinement loop, the monitoring system must detect divergence between evaluator scores and true quality over iterations.
- **ADD 4 (Reward Degradation Test Statistic)**: The Pan et al. (2022) finding that "reward hacking still occurs even when there is a positive correlation between true and proxy rewards" deepens the RewardInflationRisk driver — correlation is necessary but not sufficient for safe monitoring.
